# DHF Sponsor Sales Operations — Internal Working Playbook

**Status:** Internal working draft for owner review. Not public, not an offer, and not authorization to contact prospects.
**Brand:** Da HoodFarmer / DHF Opportunity Network.
**Scope:** All five divisions, corporate partnerships, local and regional sponsors, media partnerships, and the first $1,000 cash sponsorship target.
**Operating posture:** Free-first; no paid services, production release, live checkout, contract execution, or external prospect contact without separate owner approval.

## 1. Commercial objective and sequencing

Build one sponsor program with five coordinated inventory areas. Sell the smallest, easiest-to-deliver placements first while preparing larger corporate and media proposals in parallel.

1. Establish accurate sponsor materials, approved inventory, owner review, and a lead-tracking process.
2. Prioritize local and regional prospects with a clear fit and a short decision path for the first paid commitments.
3. Build a parallel national corporate pipeline for larger, multi-program relationships.
4. Develop media and production relationships using a deliberately non-confidential overview.
5. Deliver, document, report, renew, and expand only after each agreement is approved.

The first $1,000 target is a gross cash-receipts target, not a forecast. Preferred paths:
- Two Featured Partners at $500 each; or
- Four Community Partners at $250 each; or
- One Corporate Partner at $1,000.

Do not count in-kind value, verbal interest, unsigned proposals, unpaid invoices, or projected affiliate revenue toward the $1,000 cash target.

- Clearly label paid placement, sponsored editorial, paid interviews, affiliate relationships, and in-kind support.
- Do not sell the same exclusive inventory twice.
- No perpetual logo placement or unlimited content rights.

## 3. Five-division sponsor inventory

### A. Business Network
Potential inventory:
- Clearly labeled sponsor and partner directory profiles.
- Sponsored business spotlights or interviews.
- Small-business resource series sponsorship.
- Entrepreneur and supplier education content.
- Regional business roundtable or virtual networking segment.

Best-fit sponsor categories: banks and credit unions, accounting and legal services, insurance, business software, telecom, workforce services, logistics providers, and business-development organizations.

### B. Summits & Events
Potential inventory:
- Presenting sponsor for a defined summit or event.
- Session, workshop, or discussion sponsor.
- Digital exhibitor profile.
- Speaker/session support, where editorial and speaker independence are protected.
- Post-event sponsor report with only verified delivery data.

Best-fit categories: agricultural associations, equipment makers, technology firms, financial institutions, workforce organizations, regional utilities, and aligned consumer brands.

No event date, venue, speaker, attendance, or production capacity is confirmed unless separately verified and approved.

### C. Marketplace
Potential inventory:
- Clearly labeled featured vendor or brand profile.
- Sponsored product demonstration or comparison.
- Digital showcase placement.
- Seasonal supplier guide sponsorship.
- Vendor-market support, only after venue and operating requirements are verified.

Best-fit categories: farm and garden suppliers, tools, workwear, boots, outdoor gear, food producers, packaging, payment services, and small-business platforms.

Do not imply DHF endorses a product or independently tested it unless that is true and documented.

### D. Academy
Potential inventory:
- Sponsor-supported educational guide or workshop.
- Learning-series sponsor recognition.
- Equipment or software demonstration with disclosure.
- Scholarship or access support, with written eligibility and administration rules.
- Organizational training sponsorship.

Best-fit categories: agricultural education, universities and extension programs, workforce development, ag technology, safety training, financial education, and business software.

Do not imply accreditation, certification, government endorsement, or guaranteed outcomes without independent verification.

### E. Land & Experiences
Potential inventory:
- Sponsor-supported field day or demonstration.
- Outdoor education series sponsorship.
- Production/location partner recognition.
- Agritourism or land-stewardship program sponsorship.
- Corporate group experience support, subject to property authorization.

Best-fit categories: outdoor equipment, vehicles, workwear, insurance, agricultural suppliers, tourism organizations, and regional hospitality businesses.

No land, venue, access, or activity may be advertised as confirmed until authorized in writing and operational requirements are checked.

## 4. Prospect strategy

### First-dollar lane: local and regional
Start with businesses and organizations in the Columbia, Midlands, and wider South Carolina market that already serve agriculture, small business, outdoor recreation, education, media, or community events.

Prioritize prospects with:
- A clear audience or mission fit.
- A local decision-maker or documented sponsorship process.
- A plausible $250–$500 pilot budget.
- A deliverable DHF can fulfill now, without a new event, venue, paid tool, or unverified audience promise.
- A public business contact or official sponsorship application route.

Do not claim a relationship, endorsement, or existing partnership unless documented.

### Corporate lane: national
Build a separate target list for large agricultural equipment, seed/input, workwear, automotive, financial services, telecom/technology, food systems, and outdoor brands.

Approach national prospects with a concise corporate partnership brief and a request for the correct brand partnerships, community investment, supplier diversity, or corporate affairs contact. Do not send the confidential show concept, unreleased formats, budgets, scripts, or production mechanics.

### Media lane
Target networks, production companies, podcast studios, streaming publishers, creator agencies, and regional broadcasters for clearly defined media collaboration conversations. Keep sponsorship sales distinct from show pitching. Use only a high-level public brand description until a separate media submission and IP-protection process is approved.

## 5. Lead qualification and scoring

Score each prospect from 0 to 2 in five categories (maximum 10):
1. Brand and audience fit.
2. Ability to fund the proposed level.
3. Decision-maker/contact route identified.
4. Deliverable can be fulfilled without new spending or unapproved commitments.
5. Timing and evidence of an active sponsorship/partnership pathway.

Priority:
- 8–10: Tier A — prepare a tailored proposal for owner approval.
- 5–7: Tier B — research and nurture.
- 0–4: Tier C — hold; do not spend time on outreach.

A high score is a research priority, not permission to contact.

## 6. Sales pipeline stages

1. Research identified — source and date recorded.
2. Fit qualified — score and division assigned.
3. Contact route verified — official public route only.
4. Owner-approved for outreach — explicit approval recorded.
5. Initial contact sent — date, channel, and exact message logged.
6. Response received — classify and record next action.
7. Discovery completed — goals, budget range, timing, authority, and fit documented.
8. Proposal drafted — inventory, fee, schedule, terms, and proof of delivery specified.
9. Proposal approved by owner — no automatic authority to sign.
10. Agreement signed by authorized parties.
11. Payment received and reconciled.
12. Fulfillment in progress / completed.
13. Delivery report sent.
14. Renewal or closeout.

Never move a lead to signed, paid, or delivered based on a verbal statement.

## 8. Discovery questions

When a prospect has responded and a meeting is authorized, establish:
- What business objective is the sponsor trying to achieve?
- Which geography and audience matter?
- Which division or program is relevant?
- What budget range and approval process apply?
- What timing or campaign window matters?
- What deliverables are useful to the sponsor?
- What brand-safety, category-conflict, legal, and reporting requirements apply?
- Who has authority to approve and sign?

Do not lead with unverified audience numbers. Ask what outcome the prospect values, then match it to deliverables DHF can actually provide.

## 9. Media and production partnership boundary

For first contact, share only:
- Public brand identity and short biography.
- Public website and public social links.
- General subject areas and production interests.
- Publicly available creator/media kit materials that have been reviewed for accuracy and rights.

Do not disclose:
- Unreleased show concepts, formats, episode arcs, scripts, characters, mechanics, budgets, casting plans, sizzle materials, or production plans.
- Private business plans, partner lists, deal terms, or unreleased IP.
- Any material that is not approved for public distribution.

A general inquiry is not an IP-protection agreement. Before any confidential pitch, use a separately reviewed submission strategy, preserve dated records of original materials, check each recipient's submission terms, and obtain qualified legal advice where appropriate. Do not assume a network or producer will sign an NDA.

## 10. Sponsor delivery and reporting

For every signed sponsor:
- Store the signed agreement and approved creative.
- Record payment status separately from contract status.
- Maintain a deliverable checklist and due dates.
- Capture screenshots, links, event records, or other proof of each delivered item.
- Record campaign dates and actual measurable results only.
- Send a concise completion report.
- Obtain approval before extending, renewing, or changing benefits.
