Seasonal Farm Preparation Checklist

Inspect early, plan supplies and reduce avoidable surprises.

Seasonal preparation is a recurring management task. A short inspection before weather or workload changes can reveal repairs, supply gaps and equipment problems while there is still time to address them. Use this as a general checklist and adapt it to your location, operation and animals.

Property and infrastructure

  • Walk fences, gates, latches and high-traffic areas; record damage and needed repairs.
  • Check roofs, shelters, doors, drainage and access routes.
  • Inspect water lines, tanks, troughs and pumps for leaks, contamination or freeze exposure where relevant.
  • Clear debris from access points and drainage paths without disturbing protected areas or creating erosion.

Livestock and poultry

  • Confirm shelters provide appropriate protection and ventilation for the species and season.
  • Review feed supply, storage condition and delivery timing.
  • Check water availability and clean drinkers regularly.
  • Review animal identification, routine records and contact details for veterinary support.
  • Ask a veterinarian or extension service about seasonal health risks specific to your animals and region.

Equipment and supplies

  • Review manufacturer maintenance schedules and complete overdue service.
  • Inspect tires, batteries, lights, guards, hoses and safety equipment.
  • Check fuel and consumable supplies, storing them according to label and safety requirements.
  • Make a list of critical spare parts and repair contacts.

Budget and records

  • Compare actual feed, fuel, repair and labor costs with the budget.
  • Record upcoming purchases and seasonal service needs.
  • Check insurance, permits and relevant program deadlines directly with the responsible provider.
  • Set aside a realistic reserve for unexpected repairs and weather-related costs.

Make the checklist local

Weather patterns, soil, water availability, animal species and farm activities differ. Use local extension and agency guidance for seasonal decisions. In South Carolina, Clemson Cooperative Extension is a useful starting point for regionally relevant agricultural information; confirm current recommendations with the relevant program.

  • USDA NRCS: Solutions for Small-Scale Farms

This checklist is educational and does not replace veterinary care, equipment manuals, local regulations or site-specific technical advice.
